EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION (150-200 Words)
========================================================================================
In this clinical machine learning investigation, five supervised classification algorithms
were trained and rigorously evaluated on the Breast Cancer Wisconsin Diagnostic dataset:
Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and
Gaussian Naive Bayes. Diagnostic features extracted from digitized fine needle aspirates
(FNA) were normalized using StandardScaler to eliminate dimensional scale discrepancies.

Logistic Regression demonstrated outstanding diagnostic capability, attaining the highest
Accuracy (97.08%), Recall (93.75%), and F1-Score (96.00%) with an exemplary ROC-AUC of 0.9975.
In oncology applications, Recall is the paramount clinical metric because a False Negative
(misclassifying a malignant tumor as benign) can delay life-saving medical interventions.
Logistic Regression produced only 4 False Negatives out of 64 malignant cases, outperforming
Random Forest (6 FN) and SVM (7 FN). Furthermore, Random Forest achieved perfect Precision
(100%) and 96.49% Accuracy, demonstrating immense utility when zero false alarms are desired.
Decision Tree yielded the lowest generalization (90.06% Accuracy, 10 FN), suffering from
local greedy variance. Consequently, Logistic Regression is recommended as the premier
classification model for clinical deployment, paired with Random Forest for ensemble verification.
